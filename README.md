# Met-Strategic-Acquisition-Analysis
## Closing the Gap: Benchmarking the Met's Modern Acquisitions Against MoMA to Inform Tang Wing Strategy

## Introduction

### Business opportunity: 
The Met has publicly committed to filling the Tang Wing's 70,000+ square feet, opening in 2030, with art from artists long underrepresented in the art world. This analysis benchmarks the Met's current Modern Art acquisition composition against MoMA — providing data-driven evidence of where the Met stands on diversity, and whether its strategy is working ahead of the Tang Wing opening.

### Hypothesis: 
The Met's Modern acquisitions were expected to show underrepresentation gaps relative to MoMA — the data revealed the opposite, with MoMA showing higher Western concentration than the Met.

### Problem statement: 
How does the nationality diversity of the Met's Modern Art acquisitions compare to MoMA's, and what does that tell us about the strength of the Met's diversification strategy ahead of the Tang Wing opening in 2030?

### Methodology: 
Compares artist nationality composition of Met Modern Art acquisitions vs. MoMA acquisitions over time, using each institution's open-access collection dataset. Met open-access CSV filtered to Modern and Contemporary Art department; MoMA Artists and Artworks CSVs used for the MoMA benchmark. Wikipedia's list of most-visited art museums scraped to add public engagement context.

### Findings:
- Contrary to the initial hypothesis, the Met's Modern Art department shows greater non-Western representation than MoMA
- Met Modern Art: 82.9% Western, 17.1% Non-Western
- MoMA: 87.1% Western, 12.9% Non-Western — more Western-concentrated than the Met
- The Met receives 2.25x more annual visitors than MoMA (5.98M vs 2.66M)
- Both institutions show accelerating non-Western acquisitions post-2000
- The Met's 2010s saw 30.3% non-Western acquisitions vs MoMA's 23.6% — the Met is leading the recent diversification trend
- The data validates the Tang Wing's stated commitment to underrepresented artists

### Further Questions & Next Steps:
- Does gender representation follow similar patterns to nationality?
- Would incorporating auction/market data reveal whether diversity correlates with commercial value?
- How do other major encyclopedic museums (Smithsonian, British Museum) compare?
